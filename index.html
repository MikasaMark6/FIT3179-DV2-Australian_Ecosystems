Different landscapes, different conservation questions

Australia's vegetation mosaic supports varied habitats, recorded bird diversity and internationally important wetlands. Historical comparisons reveal different mapped patterns of change, while protection differs across regions and vegetation types. Understanding environmental diversity requires looking at where habitats occur, what remains, and how they are represented in protection — alongside the national total.

Bird data limitations and map coverage

The retained download has 6,972,714 records in 2,438 populated grids; 87.95% have unspecified coordinate uncertainty. Values are recorded richness, not a complete census, corrected richness or an estimate of conservation value. The default view includes grids whose centres are within 110–156° E and 45–9° S (2,387 grids), including nearby marine locations. The broader view includes all downloaded grid locations, including offshore and external-territory records. Occurrence counts are retained exported rows, without deduplication by occurrence ID. A missing square is not evidence of absence. The minimum-record filter hides grids; it does not recompute their species counts. See download attribution and the bird-map methods.

How to interpret the vegetation data
  1. Display: approximately 5 km cells, sampled from the NVIS 100 m raster. Small coastal patches may disappear at this scale. Map cells are not used to calculate areas. Extant data have different observation dates across regions.
  2. Area statistics use original 100 m NVIS cells in an equal-area projection. One cell equals one hectare. Percentages in tooltips use all 25 classified native groups nationwide as the denominator.
  3. Each percentage divides the group's area by all 25 classified native groups in that state. It does not measure the percentage of the state's total land. The ten displayed groups are a subset; their percentages do not sum to 100% per column. Other Territories are omitted.

DCCEEW: NVIS Version 7.0 data products — extant MVG raster. Administrative boundaries: ABS ASGS Edition 4. See the processing notes for denominators and map simplification.
